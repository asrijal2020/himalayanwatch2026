TRANSPARENCY FIRST

Evidence-based, human-authorized warning research

HimalayaWatch is an early-stage AI research prototype for multi-hazard early-warning decision support across Nepal, Tibet, India, and Pakistan. It explores how fragmented mountain and hydrometeorological evidence can become timely, traceable analysis—not automated public warnings.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Cascading hazards cross system boundaries

An ice or rock collapse can dam or displace water. A glacial lake can release suddenly. Rainfall can mobilize landslides and debris. The resulting flood can damage the very sensors needed to understand it.

The challenge is not only data scarcity: evidence arrives from different systems, on different clocks, with different uncertainty. HimalayaWatch preserves both when something was observed and when it became available, so retrospective tests do not use future knowledge.

SAFETY PRINCIPLES

What the prototype will not claim

  • Movement alone is not evidence of impending collapse.
  • Missing or stale observations are not evidence of safety.
  • Catalog access is not validated satellite hazard detection.
  • Optical scenes can confuse cloud, shadow, and seasonal snow with surface change; quicklooks are not analysis-ready products.
  • Climate change affects background conditions, but attribution for one event requires investigation.
  • Scientists validate evidence; authorized officials decide whether to issue warnings.

Prototype capabilities

  • Interpretable river-level and threshold baselines with explicit withholding states.
  • Analysis of supplied movement measurements without equating motion to failure.
  • Observation-time, availability-time, freshness, provenance, and quality checks.
  • Interactive maps, trends, downstream corridors, and traceable results.
  • Bounded discovery checks against BIPAD records and the Sentinel catalog.
  • Normalized JSON source adapters and optional Azure Event Hubs ingestion.
  • Versioned evidence receipts with payload hashes, measurement references, uncertainty, and scientific review state.
  • Workbench model-run registration with server-computed input hashes, versions, semantic checks, and withholding reasons.
  • Rasuwa–Trishuli shadow-pilot readiness gates and hazard-specific freshness limits.
  • Human-authorized parallel SMS, email, and webhook delivery with a durable, auditable outbox.
  • Historical-event and non-event evaluation path for false alarms and usable warning time.

REFERENCE CASE

26 August 2026 border flood

The application includes a retrospective representation of the supplied preliminary ICIMOD assessment: a suspected high-altitude ice-rock avalanche followed by a destructive Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood, with a reported rise of up to nine metres in 30 minutes. The trigger remains labeled preliminary, and the prototype does not make a climate-attribution claim.

This case is used to test evidence timing, sensor-loss behavior, cascading-hazard reasoning, downstream context, and warning-time evaluation. It is not presented as a live event feed.
